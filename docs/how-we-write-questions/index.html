Editorial method

How we write and check exam questions

This page describes the process, so that a claim about accuracy can be checked rather than taken on trust. It describes how we work today, on material we have built. It is not a claim about a shipped product, because we do not have one yet.

Every question is written from a named source document

For each exam we identify the documents the exam itself is set from: the regulation, the awarding body's published standards, and the official study guide where one exists. For the FAA Part 107 exam those are 14 CFR part 107, the Remote Pilot Airman Certification Standards FAA-S-ACS-10B, and the FAA Remote Pilot Study Guide FAA-G-8082-22.

Questions are written from the text of those documents. They are not collected from candidates, not scraped from other question banks, and not recalled from a real sitting. We do not publish or sell real exam questions, and we would not, because doing so breaches the awarding body's terms and teaches recall rather than understanding.

Each question records the passage it came from

A question is stored with a reference to the specific section it was drawn from, for example 14 CFR 107.51(b), together with the passage text itself. That reference travels with the question into the explanation, so a candidate who doubts an answer can go and read the rule.

This is also what makes review possible at scale. When a source document changes, the questions attached to the changed section can be found directly instead of by rereading the whole bank.

Figures are checked against the source and dated

Any number stated as fact, a fee, a pass mark, a question count, a time limit, carries the source that states it and the date it was last checked. Where the regulator publishes a figure as approximate, we repeat it as approximate rather than rounding it into something that looks more precise than it is.

A figure we cannot source does not go on the page at all. That rule is enforced by the build: the site will not compile if a published figure is not tied to a listed source.

Counts are stated honestly

Where we state the size of a question bank, the figure is the honest count of questions written for that exam. We do not inflate a bank size by counting filler, and any derived total is derived from the honest count.

We publish no download numbers, user numbers, ratings, testimonials or pass rates, because we have none. When we have them and can evidence them, we will publish them with their source, and not before.

How to report an error

If you find a mistake on a reference page or in a question, email the address below with the page and the specific figure or question. Reports about a factual error take priority over everything else we are doing.

Corrections are made to the source data and the page is rebuilt, and the check date on the affected page moves to the date of the correction.

What we do not do

We do not describe ourselves as approved, endorsed or affiliated by any regulator or awarding body, because we are not. We do not claim to be the best, the most accurate or the most up to date, because those are not statements anyone can check.

We do not publish reviews or ratings we have solicited, and we will not ask anyone to write one.
